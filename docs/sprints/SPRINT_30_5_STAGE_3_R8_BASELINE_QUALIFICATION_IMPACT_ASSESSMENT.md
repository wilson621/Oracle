## Decision

R6 package. It does not qualify the accepted Stage 2 R8 package.

Stage 3 qualification is therefore incomplete for the current R8 baseline. A new
R8-bound Stage 3 revision must execute the clean-Windows lifecycle before Stage 4,
Stage 5, production, publication, distribution or deployment can proceed.

certificate-trust, installation or qualification authority.

## Technical and qualification impact

The R6-to-R8 product correction changes foreground and placeholder presentation
across the shipped authentication and Oracle UI. It changes the immutable package
bytes even though it does not alter the established installed runtime-configuration
architecture, package-data lifecycle or network policy.

R12 proved fourteen lifecycle transitions for the R6 MSIX: transfer and host
admission, untrusted and tampered rejection, temporary trust, installation,
activation and observation, reset/repair, post-reset package-data initialisation,
second runtime-configuration consumption, repair activation and observation,
removal, trust and transfer teardown, cleanup and evidence freeze. Those claims
are package-bound. They cannot be inferred for a differently signed `0.1.6.0`
package containing changed rendered UI.

The R8 Stage 2 result proves candidate integrity and clean-host package
reconciliation. It does not replace Stage 3's installation, activation,
reset/repair, repeat-configuration, observation and teardown claims.

## Reusable protocol and non-transferable evidence

The following remain valid engineering inputs for a successor revision:

- the R12 fourteen-phase lifecycle and fail-closed acceptance criteria;
- the corrected post-reset package-root discovery and exact `LocalState` policy;
- ownership-verified activation, observation and teardown controls;
- untrusted and tampered-package rejection requirements;
- the split-host architecture in which the engineering workstation freezes the
  candidate and `Founder-QA-01` remains free of repositories and developer tools;
- create-only transfer, authority, attempt, return and evidence namespaces; and
- immutable R12 findings as historical regression requirements.

The following cannot be reused as current qualification state:

- the R12 package, certificate, candidate and transfer bindings;
- any R12 transfer, grant, authority, attempt or evidence namespace;
- R12 host admission or continuity as a substitute for fresh admission; and
- any conclusion that R12 qualified the R8 package.

## Downstream effect

Stage 4 R4 and the Stage 5 preparation remain accepted immutable history for the
R6/R12 chain only. Neither is invalidated or rewritten, but neither qualifies or
extends the R8 baseline. Stage 4 and Stage 5 remain blocked until a new R8-bound
Stage 3 result is independently reconciled and Founder-accepted.

No evidence indicates that the R8 product correction itself requires another
product change before Stage 3. The identified dependency is qualification
binding, not a new product defect.

## Recommended Founder-level mission

Authorise a bounded **Stage 3 Requalification R13 engineering-preparation
mission** against the exact accepted R8 package. It should permit protocol
adaptation, implementation, regression and adversarial validation, non-
qualification rehearsal, documentation, commit, push and qualification-readiness
preparation only.

The R13 preparation should:

1. bind the exact R8 candidate, tree, package, certificate and accepted evidence;
2. preserve every historical Stage 3 transfer, authority, attempt and evidence
   namespace unchanged;
3. retain all fourteen R12 lifecycle transitions and the corrected post-reset
   lifecycle policy;
4. preserve the established clean-host split and require no Git, Node, npm,
   Supabase CLI, Docker or development repository on `Founder-QA-01`;
5. require fresh host continuity, admission, security, zero-state and
   pre-authority gates for any later execution mission;
6. use entirely fresh identities and create-only namespaces; and
7. remain execution-barred until a later, separate Founder authorisation.
